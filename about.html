About the Project

Food Is Wasted While
People Go Hungry

FoodBridge exists to close that gap — a single place where surplus food is offered, claimed, collected and accounted for, before it spoils.

The Problem

Surplus and shortage, in the same city

Restaurants, wedding halls, hotels and households routinely end the day with good food they cannot use. Charities in the same neighbourhoods are looking for exactly that food. What is usually missing is not generosity — it is coordination.

Without a shared system, a donor has to know which NGO to ring, whether anyone can collect in time, and whether the food will still be safe when it arrives. Most of the time that is too much friction, and the food is binned instead.

Food spoils fast

Cooked surplus has hours, not days. Every donation carries an expiry time and the platform sorts the most urgent first.

No shared channel

Donors and charities rely on personal contacts. One listing here is visible to every verified NGO at once.

No accountability

Once food leaves, the donor rarely learns what happened. Here every stage is timestamped and visible to them.

No one to collect

Many charities have no spare driver. Volunteers are assigned to each pickup and tracked to the drop-off.

Our Approach

Coordination, and a record of it

Four principles the platform is built around.

Verified participants

Every account — donor, NGO or volunteer — is reviewed by an administrator before it can sign in or list anything.

Urgency first

Listings carry an expiry time, queues sort by it, and admins can flag a donation as priority when it matters more than the sort suggests.

Visible end to end

Donors follow their food through five tracked stages with real timestamps, a map, and a notification at each step.

Accountable

NGOs record what actually arrived, participants rate each other, and automated checks raise anything odd for an admin to look at.

Technology

How it is built

A conventional server-rendered web application, chosen so the whole system runs on a single machine with no paid third-party services.

Mapping uses OpenStreetMap and Nominatim rather than a commercial maps provider, so no billing account or API key is required to run it.

  • ASP.NET Web Forms on .NET Framework 4.7.2, with C# code-behind
  • SQL Server accessed through parameterised ADO.NET — no ORM
  • Bootstrap 5 with a custom stylesheet shared across every page
  • Leaflet + OpenStreetMap for maps, Nominatim for geocoding — no API key
  • PBKDF2 password hashing with a random salt per account
  • SMTP email alongside in-app notifications, with per-event preferences
  • Role-based access control enforced server-side on every page
